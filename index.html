From the Editor

A note on building, not just reporting.

Most data careers begin the same way: a spreadsheet, a deadline, and the dawning realization that the file in front of you is the source of truth for an entire team. Mine was no exception. What changed it was not a single tool or a single course, but a slow shift in posture — from describing what the numbers said, to designing the systems that produced them.

The interesting questions, I’ve learned, arrive earlier in the chain. Less “what does this number mean?” and more “why is this the number we see, and who will need to see a different one tomorrow?” The work moves accordingly — from the report on the screen to the quieter machinery underneath it. (The specifics of that machinery are catalogued on the next page.)

This issue is a small archive of how the posture was earned — from a data-ops desk in India, through a master’s in business analytics in Texas, to the present moment at Samsara, where the role on paper says analyst and the work, on most days, says something closer to builder. Lately the builder has company: AI agents that do the cleanup, flag the gaps, and answer the questions in plain English.

Read at leisure. Skim if you must. The footnotes are doing most of the work.

Project No. 01 · October 2026 · Live

AI Daily Study Notes: an agent that teaches me AI, every morning.

A Claude Skill and a GitHub Action that research the latest AI model releases, use cases, and engineering stack from primary sources, then write a 15-minute lesson with notes, runnable Python, flashcards, and a quiz. A weekly rotation (new models, tech stack, hands-on builds, use cases, review, recap) and a progress log mean no topic repeats. Each lesson is committed to GitHub and arrives as an Issue on my phone and in my inbox.

  • Claude API with server-side web search, refusal fallbacks, and long-running tool loops
  • Prompt design for accuracy: verify at the source, never invent numbers
  • Scheduled automation with GitHub Actions, secrets, auto-commits, and Issues
